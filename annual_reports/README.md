## What changed

- **Workshops remained a core service, with changing formats and reporting.** The reports list 16 online workshops in 2020–2021 and 15 in 2021–2022. The 2022–2023 report lists 23 Digital Fellows workshops and 383 registrations, which can include repeat sign-ups. It separately lists 17 workshops within the Digital Research Institute. The next two reports separate summer Python workshops from their main annual workshop count. That distinction matters when comparing 14 and 17 across the last two years. [Activity evidence](activity.csv)
- **Reported one-to-one consultations fell from 40 to 37 to 29 between 2022–2023 and 2024–2025.** That is 11 fewer sessions, or 27.5%, using the three exact community session totals. The 2020–2021 figures describe requesters and hours. The 2015–2017 report alternates among people, faculty-only sessions and a two-year headline. The report figures alone cannot explain the change in demand or availability. [Activity evidence](activity.csv)
- **Institute participation varied.** The Graduate Center Digital Research Institute moved online in 2020–2021 and offered Python and R tracks in 2021–2022. Reported cohort sizes then went from 40 to 20 to 23 to 37. The 2023–2024 report gives 25 seats and 23 participants on the same page. The 2024–2025 report uses 37 for both. The undergraduate Digital Project Institute started with 18 CUNY undergraduates in 2022–2023 and had a distinct semester-long mentoring component. [Program evidence](programs.csv) · [Activity evidence](activity.csv)
- **Core staffing continued while people moved between roles.** The five main reports list 8, 8, 8, 6 and 8 GC Digital Fellows, with 3, 5, 4 and 4 shared names between successive rosters. The last pair shares 4 of 10 distinct listed names. The 2022–2023 report lists Chelsea Lane as a postdoctoral Fellow and Param Ajmera and Di Yoong as Digital Scholarship Center Fellows after listing them as GC Digital Fellows a year earlier. Sam O’Hana appears as a GC Digital Fellow in 2023–2024 and a Data Visualization Advising Fellow in 2024–2025. These observations describe reported roles, with no inference about appointment dates or workloads. [Roster evidence](staffing.csv) · [Overlap counts](staffing_overlap.csv)
- **The reports describe overlapping routes into digital scholarship.** More than half of 2022–2023 Institute participants joined additional workshops. The 2023–2024 and 2024–2025 Digital Showcases gave Praxis students and Provost’s Digital Innovation Grant recipients a shared venue. The 2024–2025 report records the launch of Conversations in Digital Scholarship and a Humanidades Digitales blog, plus the public debut of the DHRIFT teaching platform and an April halt to its federal funding. [Program evidence](programs.csv) · [Activity evidence](activity.csv)

## What the earlier reports change

### Work visible outside the blog

The 2012–2014 report documents a portfolio that includes faculty consultations, Digital Praxis teaching, collaborative website builds and ongoing maintenance. It names Andrew McKinney as consultation lead on PDF p. 4, and Keith Miyake and Laura Kane as leads for building and maintaining the Advanced Research Collaborative Commons on p. 17. The 2015 report names the work of curating, organizing and co-teaching the workshop series on p. 5 and the website redesign on p. 24. These are specific examples of labor that a blog post count cannot measure. [Program evidence](programs.csv)

The earlier report also describes Tagging the Tower as part of teaching and outreach. It points to the *Learn Bootstrap* tutorials and reproduces a screenshot of the post *tech versus culture*. The Islamic History Commons is a second bridge across sources. Both early reports name Erin Glass as project lead, and the export contains a launch announcement. The project lead designation and the announcement’s posting-account credit are separate evidence about separate work. [Cross-source matches](archive_matches.csv)

### Earlier demand, familiar reporting limits

The 2015 report says 96% of workshops sold out and 65% had a waitlist across its first five workshop series. Its Introduction to Python example shows more than 130 students expressing interest in 60 slots. This gives early evidence of demand exceeding capacity. It leaves open how many people attended or went on to other programs. The repeated faculty-consultation figures in the two early reports describe the same three-semester claim and appear once in the dataset. Likewise, the website visitor figure repeated in the 2015 and 2015–2017 reports is a site-wide snapshot with unspecified dates. [Activity evidence](activity.csv)

## Using the files

- [Years](years.csv) is the coverage index and includes gaps and the manual-addition row.
- [Sources](sources.csv) has the report IDs, original media URLs, file hashes, physical page counts and extraction methods.
- [Activity](activity.csv) keeps units and qualifiers with each value. “As stated” records the figure as printed or explicitly derived. “More than” and “At least” preserve the reported thresholds. The 2020–2021 partial workshop-registration sum uses the 14 counts displayed on pp. 8–11.
- [Staffing](staffing.csv) records a named person and role as transcribed from report roster pages. [Staffing overlap](staffing_overlap.csv) compares adjacent documented rosters for the GC Digital Fellow role only. Name matching is exact after case and typographic normalization as needed. Role counts do not represent distinct people across different roles.
- [Programs](programs.csv) records selected program evidence, milestones and links across activities. A program’s omission from these rows leaves its status open.
- [Archive matches](archive_matches.csv) is a selected export cross-check with IDs and URLs. Run `python3 analysis/annual_reports/compare_archive.py` from the project root to regenerate the publication-mix, title-screen and roster-comparison CSVs.

To add 2025–2026, update the source register with the supplied PDF’s page count and checksum, then add page-sourced activity, staffing and program rows. Update the year index and staffing overlap from the new roster. Keep any summer activity separate when the report distinguishes it.

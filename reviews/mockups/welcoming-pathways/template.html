More filters and credit definitions

An account credit does not verify who wrote a post. Recorded author metadata and source-supported credits are different evidence levels. None establishes fellowship membership. Guest and participant posts remain included.

Search covers metadata and full saved text. All words finds every whole word in any order; Exact phrase finds adjacent whole words in one field or paragraph. Both ignore case, accents and punctuation. Draft bodies are excluded. Research/accounting: all 430 records and nine drafts’ metadata.

Posts also come from guest authors and institute participants. A guest writer may not have been a Fellow at publication. Membership, posting account and article authorship are separate records.

How does the prototype work?

Search compares words against saved titles, credits/aliases, tags and displayed excerpts. It does not search the complete original posts. Random selection gives eligible records equal chance and avoids the previous draw when another post is available.

Reading excerpts are shortened from the saved plain-text copy; images and embeds remain at the original source. The word and topic displays reuse existing NLTK/MALLET outputs. No new text model is trained or inferred here.

The back-end recipe and interface code are annotated in the source project. Data quality, original credits, uncertainty and reviewer access are preserved as design requirements.

For mentors, administrators and funders

The archive can document published work, teaching resources, participant experiences and critical reflection. It does not on its own establish program effectiveness, verified aggregate outcomes or causal contributions to the university mission. Those claims require additional evidence.

Fellows, alumni and authors should be able to find their work, understand how it is represented and request corrections. Students and faculty can follow practical questions to the original posts and GCDI resources.

Living and learning with digital change

Tagging The Tower is a blog about digital scholarship: the tools people use, the questions they ask, and how they learn and work together.

The archive brings together writing by GC Digital Fellows and other contributors, including students, institute participants and guests. It offers tutorials, reflections and accounts of scholarly practice across changing technical and institutional conditions.

Who are the Digital Fellows?

The GC Digital Fellows are one of several doctoral-student fellowship teams within GC Digital Initiatives. Based in the Digital Scholarship Lab, they support digital research and teaching through workshops, consultations and shared resources. A blog credit alone does not establish fellowship membership.

My experience of building with an AI tool

Ian G. Williams · Human-authored narrative forthcoming

This space is reserved for Ian’s first-person account: what he wanted to understand, how he worked with the AI tool, where the process helped or failed, and what responsibility remains with the people making the website.

Prompts for the process narrative
  1. Begin with a question or encounter with the archive.
  2. Describe one concrete interaction that changed your approach.
  3. Discuss an error, correction or disagreement and how you handled it.
  4. Explain what computational analysis made visible—and what it could not establish.
  5. Say what you want readers to examine, challenge or carry forward.

Do not substitute an AI-written first-person story for your experience. Exact chat logs and behavioral traces are not needed to explain this process.

1. Read the sources

Published posts supply the writing. Posting accounts, article bylines and fellowship records are different kinds of evidence.

2. Compare patterns

Saved word counts and MALLET topic models offer ways to compare texts. Cleaning choices and corpus boundaries shape those patterns.

3. Return to people and passages

Read the original posts, inspect attribution and question the interpretation. A model result is not the authors’ collective agreement.

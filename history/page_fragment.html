The explorer already had interactive analytical views, annual design presets, a random-post reader, and access to saved NLTK/MALLET profiles. Those features are background here. 01Project evolution

Make the archive discoverable

The question: How can a reader find posts by year, writer, or analyzed topic, and see who published across years?

The post index brought all 430 category records into one searchable view, with year, credited-name, status, post-type and topic controls. Results could be organized by year, credited name or strongest topic, and exported as CSV. A two-column author–year network connected published local writing to its academic years.

Read the exact request ↓

Method and implementation choices
  • Search published reading-copy text as well as titles, tags and recorded names. Keep draft bodies out of the distributable page and show their metadata with a draft label.
  • Use the saved primary MALLET model (8 topics, seed 42). Topic filtering accepts every post meeting the selected weight threshold; topic grouping uses only the strongest weight as a browsing convenience.
  • Count one author–year edge credit per distinct credited writer on each published local post. Exclude curated/source-linked records and drafts from the network while retaining their labeled credits in the index. An edge is a publication credit, not evidence of fellowship membership or collaboration.
  • Keep index choices independent of the analysis tabs, so browsing does not silently change the other charts.

What the evidence shows: The initial full network contained 60 credited local names, but its default display showed only the 20 most prolific in the selection. This display limit and incomplete body-credit extraction were separate issues.

How it was checked: Population-boundary tests checked 430 records, 421 published posts, nine drafts, topic thresholds, and collective-author edge counts. Browser checks covered search, node selection, grouping, pagination, downloads and narrow layouts.

The method behind the interface

Decisions that make the results interpretable

Keep the populations distinct

The full index, published reader, local-publication network, modeled corpus and broad credit register answer different questions. A filter result or author count is meaningful only with its scope stated. Academic-year labels run August–July. Roster matches use same-semester evidence; summer publications do not inherit fall or spring matches.

The network counts published local post credits by academic year. It does not infer cohort membership, social ties, collaboration or individual shares of collective writing.

Separate discovery from attribution

Search can find a person mentioned in a post, but a mention is not a byline. Author assignment uses explicit credits, signed contributions, source metadata or labeled provisional accounts. Literal quotations and the change ledger make corrections reviewable.

Missing roster evidence means a writer is unconfirmed by the available lists; it does not prove they were never a fellow.

Preserve topic mixtures

The index uses the saved eight-topic, seed-42 MALLET run. A topic filter checks whether a post meets the selected minimum weight. Grouping by strongest topic is a browsing convenience; a post can have several substantial topic weights.

Weights are statistical mixture components, not certainty, quality scores or exclusive themes. Topic names are interpretive labels.

Change attribution without claiming a new model fit

The author-credit revision rejoined corrected names to fixed per-post topic weights and recalculated the affected aggregates. It did not rerun NLTK cleaning or refit MALLET.

Some previously unrecognized bylines may remain in that historical cleaned corpus. Removing them in a future cleaning revision would require a new corpus version, refitting and renewed interpretation.

Make uncertainty usable

The full published-local inventory still has 249 posts provisionally attributed to their posting accounts. Readers can see the credit basis and select explicit evidence. Possible name variants remain distinct until evidence supports merging them.

The initial top-20 network display was a visibility choice. Making all authors visible fixed that issue; recovering missed bylines required a separate data review.

Keep human and tool contributions legible

The CRediT/GAIDeT page distinguishes source writers, human project direction, and delegated agent tasks. It records what the available workflow establishes, without assigning unsupported roles or claiming that automated checks are independent human verification.

Source-writing acknowledgment does not imply approval of this project or authorship of its new analysis.

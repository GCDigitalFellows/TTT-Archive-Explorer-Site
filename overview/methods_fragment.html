2. How were words and topics produced?

A corpus is the set of texts analyzed. Stopwords are common or project-specific terms removed by the saved cleaning rules. A topic model (LDA) estimates recurring word groupings; its weights are statistical descriptions, not verified meanings. k is the chosen number of topics. MALLET fitted these models; NLTK supplied language processing, word counts and phrase statistics.

The primary display uses 8 topics, seed 42. It is not declared optimal: compare k=6/8/10 and other seeds. A seed changes the model’s initial starting point; seed stability checks whether a pattern persists. Author sensitivity asks how aggregates change when a credited writer’s posts are removed; it is not a causal estimate of that person’s influence.

3. Who wrote the posts, and who was a Fellow?

A byline names a writer in a post. A byline candidate is a search result requiring review, not an accepted credit. A merged author entry groups documented name variants while preserving original strings. A cohort often means an entering group; this project instead has incomplete signed lists of active Fellows for particular semesters. Publication credits do not establish appointments, and a missing match does not establish nonmembership.

The archive also includes guest-authored posts by students who were not Fellows at publication, including participants in the Graduate Center Digital Research Institute (GCDRI). Ian supplied this contextual clarification. Guest authorship is an authorship relationship; participation and fellowship appointments are separate roles. Assess fellowship status at the post date, rather than treating it as a lifetime identity. A missing directory match alone establishes neither guest status nor nonmembership. Participant reflections remain in the full archive and its applicable analytical populations; a Fellow-only filter intentionally selects a narrower population.

4. What kind of review has happened?

Automated checks compare saved calculations and protect the baseline. Agent-run source checks verify literal excerpts. Browser checks exercise navigation. The two Codex sub-agent reviews and the supplied external persona review are simulations, not actual user studies. Ian identifies the external model as Deepseek V4.1 Flash 1017, running in the CAIL sandbox in OpenWebUI on a ZIP of an earlier iteration. The external document reports manifest-level inspection; the exact input ZIP is not recorded. Codex checked its recommendations against the current sources before implementing changes. Files named “theme coding” or “interpretation review” do not by themselves establish independent human coding. The current topic labels and prose are agent-developed; complete human confirmation is not documented.

5. How can this be reproduced or reused?

Opening index.html needs no installation. The package README gives three commands to serve and check the saved package, and separately explains the external dependencies for a full NLP refit. Such a refit must be kept as a new analysis version: historical byline handling and credits have evolved. Full retraining runtime is not benchmarked here.

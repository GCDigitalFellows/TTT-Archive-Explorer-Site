People learning, making and questioning together.

Graduate Center Digital Initiatives (GCDI) supports digital research and teaching at the Graduate Center, City University of New York (CUNY). Its GC Digital Fellows help researchers through workshops, consultations, resources and shared learning. Read the Graduate Center’s description.

Tagging the Tower is the GCDI blog represented in this export. It brings together tutorials, reflections, announcements, guest writing and recommendations from elsewhere. This companion explores those posts; it is separate from the original publication.

Why do these numbers differ?
  1. __TOTAL__ exported category records: the starting set includes __DRAFTS__ drafts.
  2. __PUBLISHED__ published records: draft metadata remains in the index, but drafts are excluded from published totals and reading copies.
  3. __LOCAL__ local/unflagged records: no external-curation marker was detected. This does not prove original authorship. The other 96 published records are curated/source-linked.
  4. __MODELED__ modeled posts: the saved analysis excludes welcome/roster posts and texts with too little eligible material, then applies its documented cleaning rules. See every inclusion and exclusion.

The __PEOPLE__ grouped author entries form a separate register across local, curated-source and draft credits. They are not a verified census of distinct people. Academic years run August–July; 2026–27 is partial. Export snapshot: November 1, 2026.

An interpretation to explore

Digital scholarship as a collective practice

Learning to use a tool is part of the story. So are finding collaborators, repairing access to research, setting limits and deciding what deserves to be built.

The source-linked essay develops six themes around purpose, shared learning, care, ethics, AI and convening. It is an AI-assisted qualitative synthesis of 19 selected posts, with named human writers and inspectable source passages. It does not establish program-wide effectiveness or causal differences between cohorts.

From export to interpretation

How the project works

  1. Keep the recordParse the WordPress export; distinguish publication status, local writing and curated sources.
  2. Recover the creditsReview bylines and signed sections; distinguish writers from posting accounts; document supported name merges.
  3. Connect the contextOrganize posts by academic year and link writers to available same-semester Fellow rosters.
  4. Compare and readCount words with NLTK and estimate recurring word groupings with MALLET, then read the original texts to interpret the patterns.

The browser explores saved results. It does not run or refit NLTK or MALLET. Missing Fellow lists, uncertain authorship and selected testimony limit what can be inferred. Images, embeds, formatting and linked artifacts are not analyzed as text; original posts may carry arguments the reading copy cannot reproduce.

About this excerpt

How are these excerpts selected?

The starting collection contains 30 exact excerpts from 25 articles, selected with AI assistance at Ian G. Williams’s direction for curiosity, recognition, emotional connection and meaningful ideas. Joy, humor, uncertainty, care and unfinished work belong here alongside practical advice and critical reflection. These choices are editorial judgments, informed by simulated audience perspectives and inclusive-design guidance, rather than measured reader preferences. They have not received comprehensive independent human review.

Broader random discovery keeps 1631 mechanically eligible sentences from 90 source-supported local articles. NLTK identifies literal spans, filtered to 12–45 words and at most 300 characters with source-markup, notice, repetition and context heuristics. Those fallible rules can miss fragments or historical invitations. Neither collection represents the whole archive.

Each eligible article has an equal chance, then one eligible excerpt is chosen within it. A longer article receives no extra article tickets. Each collection keeps its own article-ID cycle in this tab’s session; articles do not repeat until that pool is exhausted. Switching collections avoids the article just shown when alternatives exist. Reload draws again; in-app navigation keeps the sentence. No text, author names or visitor identity is saved by this feature. If session storage is unavailable, reload history is lost.

Read in context highlights the literal sentence with the full saved paragraph, source credits, links and separate rights. Credits describe the article, preserving coauthors and the distinction between article writers and quoted speakers. No quotation is rewritten; explanations sit outside its text.

For mentors, administrators and funders

The archive can document published work, teaching resources, participant experiences and critical reflection. It does not on its own establish program effectiveness, verified aggregate outcomes or causal contributions to the university mission. Those claims require additional evidence.

Fellows, alumni and authors should be able to find their work, understand how it is represented and request corrections. Students and faculty can follow practical questions to the original posts and GCDI resources.

      Methods and cautions for year-exclusive vocabulary

      For every retained cleaned form, Python records the set of academic years containing it. A set of size one qualifies. All 5,731 qualifying forms are saved without a new frequency threshold. The evidence selector distinguishes single-post from multi-post forms; it does not redefine exclusivity. Counts, repeated occurrences and contributing posts are separate quantities. Years are ordered chronologically; within each year, table order is descending occurrences, then containing posts, then alphabetically. Search is a case-insensitive literal substring over these normalized forms.

      The definition is fixed against all 15 saved academic years even when one year is selected. Names, code identifiers, spelling noise and preprocessing artifacts can qualify. A form used in several posts may still come from the same author. More posts and longer texts give more opportunities to observe vocabulary. 2026–2027 is partial with three analyzed posts. The companion is separate from the sunburst hierarchy because type exclusivity does not subdivide its topic weights or selected-word counts. No NLTK cleaning, MALLET fit, authorship or population change is made. Detailed method and attribution.

      How to read these views · analysis and methods

      The word cloud ranks by occurrences, containing posts, or the positive percentage-point gap between one year’s post share and all other years’ post share. The last option requires two or more matching posts that year and is separate from the sunburst’s smoothed token score. Select a word to follow its yearly post shares, read short exact-form excerpts, or pick a related post. The count table gives exact denominators.

      Worked example (invented counts): if “archive” occurs in 6 of 20 posts in one year and 10 of 100 posts in the other years, the cloud's distinctive gap is 30% minus 10% = 20 percentage points. The sunburst uses a different, smoothed token-rate score: wedge size is a share of selected scores, not a percentage of posts. Neither calculation is a significance test.

      Choose a lens to change the question. Shared vocabulary counts a word once per containing post; its wedges divide all word–post incidences, including Other words. It does not divide distinct posts because each post contains many words. Subject mixtures sums the eight saved MALLET weights: each post contributes one unit across topics. Topic-first and year-first hierarchies preserve those same contributions. Topic labels remain interpretations.

      Distinctive vocabulary compares each year's token rates against the other years, with smoothing and minimum counts. Five positive log₂ rate-ratio scores divide each year; these are score shares, not percentages of writing. The research guide records thresholds and a stricter-threshold sensitivity comparison. Adjacent-word phrases counts actual neighbouring content-word pairs from saved readable text, without joining words across removed words or punctuation. Raw frequency retains repeated token counts for comparison. Phrase and raw modes include their remaining counts as Other.

      Shared, phrase, raw and distinctive modes give each eligible year equal space. Topic-mode years have space proportional to modeled posts. Year labels remain visible; smaller outer labels may be omitted, with their names and numbers always in the exact table and source panel. Select a year to expand its branches or use Show all academic years. The heatmap compares a common set of words/topics across all years using the same underlying calculations, with exact numbers in every cell. A dash means unavailable, not zero. It excludes Other and uses the union of the five displayed terms per year, or all eight topics.

      The builder sums the saved NLTK-cleaned token files, joined to current post records by ID. It uses all retained tokens before MALLET’s additional vocabulary pruning. The historical recipe removes repeated boilerplate and introductory credit lines, tokenizes words, lowercases, normalizes possessives, applies part-of-speech-aware WordNet lemmatization, then removes English and documented custom stopwords and short tokens. A lemma groups forms such as plural and singular; these English-language guesses can be wrong. Read source text for context, negation and multilingual writing.

      The saved manifest includes 301 of 423 published records; curated/source-linked posts, welcome/roster material, exact cleaned duplicates and items below the 30-cleaned-token threshold are excluded by the documented recipe. Drafts are excluded. Academic years use the saved August–July assignment; 2026–2027 is partial in the November 9, 2026 export. No later years or missing observations are invented. Raw counts weight longer posts more heavily; differences may reflect post volume, genre, author mix, exclusions and incomplete years. Frequency does not establish a theme, consensus, impact or fellowship membership. Word and phrase frequencies remain separate from the essay’s interpretive themes; Subject mixtures explicitly uses the saved MALLET model.

      No NLTK cleaning or MALLET fitting runs in the browser. Python aggregates saved tokens, extracts literal phrase candidates and calculates versioned distinctiveness scores during the build; controls select saved results. Ian G.       How the explorer was built

      Search compares words and phrases against saved titles, credits/aliases, tags and complete saved plain-text reading copies. Images, embeds and original formatting are outside that search. Random selection gives eligible records equal chance and avoids the previous draw when another post is available.

      Reading excerpts are shortened from the saved plain-text copy; images and embeds remain at the original source. The word and topic displays reuse existing NLTK/MALLET outputs. No new text model is trained or inferred here.

      Project workflow diagrams and text versions show how the explorer was built. The analysis recipe and interface code are annotated in the private source project. Data quality, original credits, uncertainty and reviewer access are preserved as design requirements.

      What did the cleaning audit find?

      A targeted check of six saved posts compared readable text, cleaned tokens and the institution-related stop list. In “Doing humanidades digitales at CUNY,” the saved cleaned text retains “humanidades digitales” and “español,” while removing institution labels such as CUNY and fellow. This is a documented recipe choice, not evidence that the removed words lack meaning.

      English grammar processing is not a multilingual language assessment. This small audit does not validate translation, grammatical tags or topic quality. The original reading text remains available. The separate cleaning-comparison preview compares already fitted recipes; these checks did not fit another model.
